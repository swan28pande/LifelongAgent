## External primary evidence

| Source observation | Primary source | Limit for this question |
|---|---|---|
| HorizonBench tests updated preferences inferred from life events over six-month synthetic histories: 360 users, 4,245 five-choice items, about 163K tokens per history. Its best tested full-history model scored 52.8%. | [HorizonBench §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17283) | Items were filtered for difficulty; human majority accuracy was 56–64%. It does not establish universal failure on natural open-ended assistance or newer models. |
| PERMA inserts preference-related queries at points along multi-session, event-driven histories and includes interactive tasks. | [PERMA §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23231) | Synthetic users and simulated feedback; not exact daily-choice forecasting. |
| PersonaMem-v2 studies mostly implicit preferences, updates, and forgetting across synthetic user histories. | [PersonaMem-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06688) | Different output format and preference definition from the local choice timeline. |
| MemOps grades explicit remember, update, forget, and reflect operation traces with supporting evidence. Memora penalizes obsolete memory. | [MemOps §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12893), [Memora](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20006) | These are separate benchmarks; neither is a measured comparison with `memory_v3`. |
| Mem2ActBench tests using remembered information in tool-call arguments; PersonalAlign tests vague GUI instructions and proactive routines from long-term records. | [Mem2ActBench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.370/), [PersonalAlign](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1669/) | Their action environments differ from the local chat agent. |
| CAPTURE explicitly separates genuine preference drift from temporary context and poisoning, with uncertainty-triggered clarification. | [CAPTURE abstract and method](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02265) | September 2026 preprint; its reported results depend on its evaluation setup. |
| LongMemEval-V2 tests memory over 25M–115M-token web-agent histories. RealTalk uses 21 days of authentic human conversations. MemoryCD studies real user behavior over longer periods. | [LongMemEval-V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12493), [RealTalk](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13270), [MemoryCD](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25973) | They test different sources, tasks, and outcomes; no cross-paper ranking is justified. |
| Forecasting preferences and adaptive recommendations have their own research lineage. | [LATTE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26612), [Li et al., contextual bandits (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0146) | Forecasting reviews/clicks is not identical to conversation-based choice prediction. |

## Interpretation drawn from that evidence

1. The local corpus can check that extraction, storage, summary construction, and answering run. Its one person, three fixed rules, repeated daily cues, and answer imbalance make it too narrow to measure generalization of a lifelong agent memory architecture.
2. A successful full-history model on this corpus would remove an accuracy-based argument for the current hierarchy **on this corpus**. It would leave measurable questions about online updates, future decisions, evidence fidelity, and total lifecycle cost.
3. Generic novelty claims for temporal ledgers, hierarchical summaries, preference evolution, operation traces, and proactive personalization are weak because inspected primary work already treats each. A contribution must be defined by a specific capability and measured against matched baselines.
