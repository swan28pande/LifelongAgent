You write one day's chat between a personal AI assistant and its user. The chat will be
stored in the assistant's long-term memory, and questions about the user's life will later
be graded against a hidden ground truth. Your only job is to render the facts you are given
into a natural conversation. Accuracy matters more than style.

THE CONVERSATION
- 12 to 20 turns, alternating between "assistant" and "user". Either may speak first.
- It should read like a real daily check-in: casual, specific, a little meandering. The
  user sounds like a real person, not a form being filled in.
- The assistant remembers earlier conversations. It may refer to anything under WHAT THE
  ASSISTANT ALREADY KNOWS and may ask about the FOLLOW-UP TOPICS. It must never state or
  guess what the user chose today; the user says it first.

TODAY'S CHOICES
- The user mentions every topic listed under TODAY'S CHOICES and states the value clearly,
  as something they did or are doing today.
- Follow the WORDING LEVEL for how the value is phrased.
- The user reports only today's choice. They never describe a schedule, cycle, rotation or
  rule behind it ("I alternate every week", "Mondays are always…"), and never say what they
  will choose on other days.
- For a one-off exception, the user says they did something different today and gives the
  reason. It must read as a one-off, not a change of habit.
- Topics listed under MUST NOT COME UP are not mentioned at all, by either speaker, not even
  in passing or as a question.

REQUIRED UPDATES
- The user states every required update explicitly, and the conversation spends 2 to 4
  turns on each (the assistant reacts or asks a follow-up question).
- Keep every update on the day it is given. If it happened today, it happened today; if it
  is marked as having happened earlier, the user describes it in the past and says roughly
  when ("last Tuesday", "a few days ago"), consistent with that timing.
- Facts about other people are clearly about that person, never about the user.

CAUSES — follow each CAUSE HANDLING instruction exactly
- LINK: the user says their routine for that topic has changed and explicitly gives the
  stated reason as the cause.
- NO LINK: the event and the routine may both appear, but nobody connects them. The user
  does not say the routine changed, and neither speaker suggests the event affected it.
- NO REASON: the user mentions today's choice without giving or hinting at any reason for
  it, and the assistant does not offer one.

DISTRACTORS
- Mention each distractor naturally as news or small talk. Nobody connects it to the
  user's own routines.

NEVER INVENT
- Do not introduce any lasting fact about the user that is not listed: no new people, pets,
  jobs, moves, hobbies, health events, trips, purchases or plans.
- Do not add backstory to known facts: no durations or start dates ("for three years",
  "since last month", "a few weeks ago"), no history of how things began, no new details
  about people or pets already known.
- Everyday texture is fine and encouraged: how the day is going, work being busy, the
  weather, what they might do this evening, how they feel.
- Do not contradict anything in WHAT THE ASSISTANT ALREADY KNOWS.

Return only JSON:
